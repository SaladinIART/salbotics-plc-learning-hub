1. Operating Modes: Manual, Semi-Auto, Auto

the same machine behaves differently depending on mode ownership

Manual

Operator or technician commands one device at a time: jog conveyor, open valve, start pump, inch press, or pulse a feeder. Manual is for testing, clearing, setup, and maintenance support. Safety, permissives, overloads, pull-cords, pressure limits, and machine-damage interlocks must still block unsafe movement.

  • Example: jog FFB conveyor only while pull-cord healthy and downstream chute clear.
  • Operator chooses device; PLC checks if it is allowed.
  • Never use manual mode to bypass lockout/tagout or certified safety circuits.

Semi-Auto

Operator starts one station or one recipe step, then the PLC finishes that step using timers, sensors, and permissives. Semi-auto is common during commissioning, batch cycles, sterilizer cycle start, transfer pump cycle, and station-by-station training.

  • Example: press START CYCLE and sterilizer runs fill steam, hold, exhaust, drain.
  • Operator confirms the next step or next batch.
  • Good for learning because the PLC sequence is visible and bounded.

Auto

The PLC or PAC coordinates a full station or line automatically. Upstream equipment waits for downstream ready. Conveyors start downstream first, feeders start last, and faults stop only the affected area unless a plant-wide trip is required.

  • Example: empty fruit conveyor, thresher, fruit elevator, digester feed run as one train.
  • SCADA shows request, ready, running, fault, hold, and alarm states.
  • Auto needs good naming, handshakes, and alarms or troubleshooting becomes painful.

2. Main Process Flow

fresh fruit bunches to crude palm oil

Beginner process meaning

FFBFresh fruit bunches arrive by truck, are weighed, and move into the mill.
SterilizerSteam softens fruit, reduces enzyme activity, and prepares bunches for separation.
PressHigh motor load extracts oil from digested fruit mash.
ClarifierHeat, dilution, and settling separate oil from water, solids, and sludge.
CPOCrude palm oil is dried, cleaned, and stored before dispatch or refinery transfer.

Automation meaning

Digital I/OMotor run feedback, valve open/close, limit switches, level switches, trips.
Analog I/OPressure, temperature, flow, tank level, motor current, speed reference.
VFDControls conveyors, press, pump, fan, and feeder speed where variable control is useful.
HMI/SCADAOperator screens, alarms, trends, recipes, production counters, shift reports.
HistorianStores sterilizer cycles, press current, clarifier temperature, CPO tank level, and alarms.

3. Conveyor And Handling Systems

where many real plant faults begin

Conveyor start/stop rule

StartStart downstream first, then upstream. This prevents feeding material into a stopped conveyor or blocked chute.
StopStop upstream first, then let downstream run for a cleanout delay.
TripPull-cord, belt drift, zero-speed, blocked chute, overload, and E-stop must stop related upstream equipment.
RestartRequire fault reset, local inspection, and downstream-ready confirmation before restarting.

5. Four Mixed Control Stations

economy PLC, PAC, Raspberry Pi industrial edge, Arduino industrial PLC

Station 1: WECON

Use for affordable machine control, local HMI, VFDs, and simple remote support. Strong learning fit for small conveyors and pump panels.

Station 2: PAC

Use PAC-class control where uptime, diagnostics, scalable I/O, process control, motion, and enterprise integration matter.

Station 3: Raspberry Pi

Use industrial Pi hardware as an edge gateway, vision/AI node, protocol bridge, dashboard device, or local data collector.

Station 4: Arduino

Use Opta or Portenta Machine Control for fast prototyping, learning IEC 61131-3, small skids, lab rigs, and controlled pilots.

AI at the edge

Do anomaly detection on motor current, boiler trend changes, camera quality checks, vibration features, or energy dashboards.

Safety boundary

Safety functions need risk assessment and certified safety components. Do not replace a safety PLC with a normal SBC or hobby board.

7. PAC And IIoT Control Network

keep control, safety, edge, and cloud roles separated

Protocol roles

OPC UAStructured industrial data model from PLC/PAC/SCADA to clients, with security and subscriptions.
MQTTLightweight publish/subscribe messaging for remote or bandwidth-limited IIoT data paths.
SparkplugMQTT convention for industrial state awareness, birth/death certificates, and tag discovery.
HistorianStores time-series plant data for troubleshooting, quality, energy, and audit evidence.

Network design habits

SegmentSeparate field/control, edge/DMZ, office IT, and cloud paths.
Least accessLet dashboards read tags; do not let cloud systems directly command critical outputs.
Time syncUse consistent timestamps for alarms, trends, batches, and event investigation.
BackupKeep PLC/PAC project backups, HMI files, network drawings, and parameter lists.

9. Technician Fault Scenarios

what to check before editing logic

Sterilizer pressure fault

Check transmitter range, impulse line, steam supply, valve feedback, pressure relief status, and interlock permissives.

Press overload

Check jammed feed, screw wear, VFD current, motor bearing, phase loss, drive fault code, and upstream digester condition.

Clarifier temperature low

Check steam valve command, actual valve travel, temperature transmitter, condensate trap, and heating coil fouling.

CPO tank high level

Check pump availability, valve lineup, level transmitter scaling, high-high switch, transfer permissive, and dispatch status.

Boiler low pressure

Check fuel conveyor, fan VFD, feedwater control, steam demand, blowdown state, and boiler trip summary.

Sensor wiring fault

Check NPN/PNP match, input common, 24V supply, shield, terminal looseness, wrong channel, and forced bits.
